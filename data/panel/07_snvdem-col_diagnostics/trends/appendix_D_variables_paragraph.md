### Appendix D (revised, 4 paragraphs)

**¶1 -- Pre-benchmark variables:**
The final dataset of subnational democracy in Colombia contains 26,928 observations (1,122
municipalities x 24 years). The pre-benchmark stage produces five variables: Municipal code
(MPIO_CDPMP), Year (year), Subnational Electoral Fairness (snelect), Subnational Civil Liberties
protections (sncivlib), and Subnational Democracy Index (sndem) — the simple mean of snelect and
sncivlib. All three indices are on the 0-1 internal CDF scale described above; for our
within-Colombia validation analyses we use them normalized to a 0-1 scale.

**¶2 -- Pre-benchmark figures:**
Figure D.1 shows the national average trajectory of these pre-benchmark scores over time. Figure D.2
disaggregates this trajectory into deciles, showing how the gap between Colombia's highest- and
lowest-performing municipalities has widened or narrowed over the period. Figures D.3 and D.4 show
the full within-year distribution behind these summary trends — density plots, by year, of the
electoral fairness (snelect) and civil liberties (sncivlib) component indices, respectively.

**¶3 -- Benchmarking + calibration figures:**
The benchmarking stage (see "Calibrating Data for Cross-National Comparisons") recalibrates these two
components against V-Dem's national-level Colombia estimates. EL_col_mt and CL_col_mt anchor each
municipality to V-Dem's national subnational mean for that year (v2elffelr for elections; CLSNmean
for civil liberties) and scale its deviation by the national range implied by V-Dem's own
"unevenness" questions (Eqs. 4-6). Figures D.5 and D.6 show this step directly: each municipality's
calibrated position plotted against V-Dem's implied national range for that year, confirming that
municipal variation stays within the envelope V-Dem's own expert-coded assessments imply.

**¶4 -- Benchmarked variables + figures:**
EL_col_mt and CL_col_mt are further rescaled onto "global standard deviation units" — EL_col_gz and
CL_col_gz — using a fixed mean and SD estimated from the full 2000-2023 V-Dem country-year panel, so
that Colombian municipalities are comparable in principle to subnational units from any other
country. sndem_final, the simple mean of EL_col_gz and CL_col_gz, is the benchmarked counterpart to
sndem and the measure used in cross-national comparisons (Eq. 1). Figures D.7 and D.8 show the
resulting benchmarked distributions, again by year — the cross-nationally comparable counterparts to
Figures D.3 and D.4.

The final dataset of subnational democracy in Colombia contains 26,928 observations (1,122
municipalities × 24 years), **produced in three stages: weighting geopredictor data by V-Dem expert
responses, calibrating the result against V-Dem's own national-level estimates for Colombia, and
rescaling that calibration onto a scale comparable across countries. We describe each stage and its
resulting variables in turn below, so readers can see exactly how the method's raw inputs become
both a within-Colombia measure and a globally comparable one.**

**Stage 1: Weighted geo-predictor data**

This stage, which weights geopredictor data by expert responses from the V-Dem survey, ultimately
produces five core variables for our measure: Municipal code (MPIO_CDPMP), Year (year), Subnational
Electoral Fairness (snelect), Subnational Civil Liberties protections (sncivlib), and Subnational
Democracy Index (sndem)—the simple mean of snelect and sncivlib. All three indices are on the 0-1
internal CDF scale described above; for our within-Colombia validation analyses we use them
normalized to a 0-1 scale. Figure D.1 shows the national average trajectory of these pre-benchmark
scores over time. Figure D.2 disaggregates this trajectory into deciles, showing how the gap between
Colombia's highest- and lowest-performing municipalities has widened or narrowed over the period.
Figures D.3 and D.4 show the full within-year distribution behind these summary trends—density
plots, by year, of the electoral fairness (snelect) and civil liberties (sncivlib) component
indices, respectively.

Figure D.2. Annual mean subnational democracy scores by decile
Figure D.3. Density plot of subnational electoral freeness and fairness scores per year
Figure D.4. Density plot of subnational civil liberty strength scores per year

**Stage 2: Calibration for cross-national comparison**

The subsequent stage (see "Calibrating Data for Cross-National Comparisons") then recalibrates the
two components against V-Dem's national-level Colombia estimates. EL_col_mt and CL_col_mt anchor
each municipality to V-Dem's national subnational mean for that year (v2elffelr for elections;
CLSNmean for civil liberties) and scale its deviation by the national range implied by V-Dem's own
"unevenness" questions (Eqs. 4-6). Figures D.5 and D.6 depict how each municipality's calibrated
position plotted against V-Dem's implied national range for that year, confirming that municipal
variation stays within the range set by V-Dem's own expert-coded assessments.

**Stage 3: Global benchmarking**

EL_col_mt and CL_col_mt are further rescaled onto "global standard deviation units"—EL_col_gz and
CL_col_gz—using a fixed mean and SD estimated from the full 2000-2023 V-Dem country-year panel, so
that Colombian municipalities are comparable in principle to subnational units from any other
country. sndem_final, the simple mean of EL_col_gz and CL_col_gz, is the benchmarked counterpart to
sndem and the measure used in cross-national comparisons (Eq. 1). Figure D.7 shows the resulting
benchmarked municipal averages—the cross-nationally comparable counterpart to Figure D.1. Figures
D.7 and D.8 show the annual benchmarked distributions—comparable counterparts to Figures D.3 and D.4.

## Graphics decision: why NOT an overlaid pre/post density plot

Checked both a native-scale dual-axis overlay and a normalized (0-1 min-max) overlay of
snelect-vs-EL_col_gz and sncivlib-vs-CL_col_gz (pooled across all 24 years). Findings:

- On a fair, comparable (0-1 normalized) basis, dispersion is essentially unchanged post-benchmark
  for elections (SD 0.180 pre vs. 0.178 post) and *higher*, not lower, for civil liberties
  (0.163 -> 0.200) and the composite (0.178 -> 0.194) -- recomputed 2026-08-09 post the CLSNyrmean
  fix above; same conclusion as the pre-fix numbers (0.195/0.191), just slightly more pronounced.
  The "pre-benchmark looks more dispersed"
  impression from eyeballing the two variable families on their native axes is a scale artifact,
  not a real loss of information from benchmarking.
- What IS real: within a single year, the benchmarked distributions are taller/narrower (e.g. peak
  density ~8-9 for CL_col_gz vs. ~6-7 for sncivlib), while between years the year-clusters separate
  out much more distinctly than pre-benchmark's mostly-overlapping year curves. Civil liberties
  specifically shows genuine within-year compression in 2010-2023 (SD ratio 0.5-0.8 vs.
  pre-benchmark), consistent with V-Dem's assessed subnational "unevenness" for Colombian civil
  liberties narrowing over that period.
- A literal overlay (same axis, or dual-axis) invites readers to compare curve widths that aren't
  measuring the same thing (within-Colombia CDF percentile vs. cross-nationally-anchored global-SD
  position) -- checked this directly and it visually misleads in both directions depending on which
  axis framing is used. Recommendation: keep D.3/D.4 (pre) and D.3b/D.4b (post) as separate
  small-multiples on their own native axes, not overlaid.
