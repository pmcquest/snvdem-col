In 01_benchmark.R, elections and civil liberties are supposed to be centered the same
way: each municipality's score is expressed as a deviation from its dimension's own
cross-municipality mean, then rescaled onto the V-Dem anchor. For elections (line 63):

    snelectyrmean <- mean(snelect, na.rm = TRUE)   # cross-municipality mean of snelect
    EL_col_mt = v2elffelr + (snelect - snelectyrmean) * weighted_range / ELrange_975_025

But the civil-liberties equivalent (line 62) isn't the CL analog of that line:

    CLSNyrmean <- mean(CLSNmean, na.rm = TRUE)     # CLSNmean is already a national constant
    CL_col_mt = CLSNmean + (sncivlib - CLSNyrmean) * wtdCL_range / CLrange_975_025

CLSNmean is constant across every municipality within a year (it's the national anchor
itself), so CLSNyrmean just re-computes that same constant. The line ends up subtracting
the national anchor from the municipal predictor (sncivlib), not the municipal
predictor's own cross-sectional mean from itself — which is a different centering than
what elections gets.

Swapping in mean(sncivlib, na.rm = TRUE) for CLSNyrmean
makes the municipal mean of CL_col_mt match CLSNmean exactly, every year (current max
gap: 0.052 in raw CL_col_mt units; corrected: 0) — mirroring how elections already
behaves. This is the dominant source of the residual gap between CL_col_mt and its
anchor in our diagnostics (I'd previously assumed that gap was just qnorm() nonlinearity, but it's not).

This is inherited from your original COL_benchmarked.R (lines 33-34), not something introduced in my revision. I don't see anything in the
operational strategy doc that explains why CL and elections would be centered
differently, but I want to confirm
with you before changing it.
